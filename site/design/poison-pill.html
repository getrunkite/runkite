Crash loops must end.

Generation fencing stops a late runner from winning. The poison pill stops a crash-looping job from being dispatched forever. At reclaim.max_retries, no further attempt is enqueued. The current assignment stays recoverable until terminal persistence and finalization succeed.

What “poison” means here

Not content filtering. A job that keeps killing runners (OOM, bad tool, broken graph) would otherwise bounce reclaim → dequeue → crash → reclaim… forever, burning capacity and drowning operators in noise. 

attempt 1  dequeue · crash · reclaim   generation++
attempt 2  dequeue · crash · reclaim   generation++
attempt 3  dequeue · crash · next reclaim would be 4th

ceiling (default 3):  retain attempt 3 · reject next generation 4
                         recover its delivered terminal, or commit error
                         finalize · Ack generation 3 · do not re-enqueue

Config

  • reclaim.max_retries in the control plane's first discovered config (default 3).
  • Missing field → default 3. Explicit 0 → unlimited (legacy escape).
  • Env override at process start: RUNKITE_RECLAIM_MAX_RETRIES.

Applies on the in-process, Redis, NATS and Kafka queues; their existing support tiers are unchanged. A poison record names the rejected next generation. finishPoisonPill acknowledges the retained generation only after completion and finalization succeed. Database or finalization failure leaves its lease for the next tick. This is separate from live cancel and Admin kill switches.

Source: finishPoisonPill / reclaimStaleJobs.

How it pairs with fencing

Fencing answers: “which attempt is allowed to write?” The poison pill answers: “how many attempts are allowed at all?” You need both, otherwise a well-fenced crash loop still never dies.

Exhausting retries stops dispatch, while a storage outage cannot silently discard the assignment that still needs a durable outcome.
